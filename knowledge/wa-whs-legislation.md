# Western Australia — Work Health and Safety Legislative Framework

> **Purpose.** This reference describes the Western Australian work health and safety (WHS) **legislative framework** — the Acts and Regulations that create enforceable duties — for both general industry and mining. It is written for citation accuracy: every clause number below was checked against an authoritative source (legislation.wa.gov.au, worksafe.wa.gov.au / commerce.wa.gov.au, dmirs.wa.gov.au). Where a specific number or amount could not be verified from those sources, it is marked **[unverified]**.
>
> **Status note.** WA adopted the national "model" WHS laws. The *Work Health and Safety Act 2020* and its three sets of supporting Regulations all commenced on **31 March 2022**, replacing the earlier *Occupational Safety and Health Act 1984* regime and (for mining) the operative safety provisions of the *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994*.

---

## 1. Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA)

**Short title:** *Work Health and Safety Act 2020* (WA)
**Act number / assent:** No. 36 of 2020; assented to **10 November 2020** (passed by Parliament 3 November 2020).
**Commencement:** **31 March 2022** (see s. 2(1)(c) and SL 2022/18 cl. 2). The WHS Act and all three sets of Regulations came into effect on this single date.

The WHS Act is the umbrella statute: it applies to **all workplaces** in WA, including general industry, mines, and petroleum and geothermal energy operations. It is closely aligned with the national model WHS laws (also adopted by NSW, QLD, SA, TAS, ACT, NT and the Commonwealth).

### 1.1 Regulator

The **WorkSafe Commissioner** is the regulator under the WHS Act. The Commissioner is responsible to the Minister for Industrial Relations for administration of the WHS Act and other WHS laws. The regulator is **WorkSafe WA** (within the Department of Energy, Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety — DMIRS). The Act also provides for a **Chief Inspector of Mines** (replacing the former State Mining Engineer) with prescribed functions (Schedule 1, Division 2).

### 1.2 Primary duty of care — the PCBU (s. 19)

The central duty holder is the **person conducting a business or undertaking (PCBU)**.

**s. 19(1)** — a PCBU must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health and safety of:
- workers engaged, or caused to be engaged, by the person; and
- workers whose activities in carrying out work are influenced or directed by the person,

while the workers are at work in the business or undertaking.

**s. 19(2)** — a PCBU must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that the health and safety of **other persons** is not put at risk from work carried out as part of the conduct of the business or undertaking.

**s. 19(3)** — without limiting the above, a PCBU must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable:
- (a) a work environment without risks to health and safety;
- (b) safe plant and structures;
- (c) safe systems of work;
- (d) safe use, handling, storage and transport of plant, structures and substances;
- (e) adequate facilities for worker welfare;
- (f) provision of necessary information, training, instruction or supervision;
- (g) monitoring of workers' health and workplace conditions to prevent illness/injury.

The duty is to **eliminate** risks to health and safety, or if not reasonably practicable, **minimise** them so far as is reasonably practicable (s. 17 — duty to manage risks). "So far as is reasonably practicable" is defined in s. 18.

### 1.3 Duty of officers — due diligence (s. 27)

**s. 27** — if a PCBU has a duty or obligation under the Act, an **officer** of the PCBU must exercise **due diligence** to ensure the PCBU complies with that duty or obligation.

"Officer" takes its meaning from s. 9 of the *Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)* (other than partners in a partnership) — broadly, a person who makes or participates in making decisions affecting the whole or a substantial part of the organisation's activities.

Due diligence includes taking reasonable steps to: acquire and keep up-to-date knowledge of WHS matters; understand the operations and their hazards/risks; ensure appropriate resources and processes are available and used; ensure processes exist to receive and respond to incident/hazard/risk information; ensure compliance processes are implemented; and verify the provision and use of those resources and processes.

An officer may be charged with an offence whether or not the PCBU is convicted.

### 1.4 Duty of workers (s. 28)

**s. 28** — while at work, a worker must:
- (a) take reasonable care for their own health and safety;
- (b) take reasonable care that their acts or omissions do not adversely affect others' health and safety;
- (c) comply, so far as reasonably able, with any reasonable instruction given by the PCBU to allow the PCBU to comply with the Act; and
- (d) cooperate with any reasonable PCBU policy or procedure relating to health or safety that has been notified to workers.

Other persons at the workplace have a parallel duty under **s. 29**.

### 1.5 Consultation duties (Part 5)

- **s. 46** — Duty to consult with other duty holders (consult, cooperate and coordinate).
- **s. 47** — Duty to consult workers.
- **s. 48** — Nature of consultation (share information; give workers a reasonable opportunity to express views and contribute; take views into account; advise of the outcome in a timely manner).
- **s. 49** — When consultation is required (e.g. identifying hazards and assessing risks, deciding risk-control measures, proposing changes, and other prescribed matters).

### 1.6 Incident notification — notifiable incidents

- **s. 37** — meaning of "notifiable incident" (the death of a person, a serious injury or illness, or a dangerous incident arising out of the conduct of a business or undertaking). **[Section number for the definition verified via WorkSafe/AustLII; confirm exact wording against the Act.]**
- **s. 38** — Duty to notify the regulator of a notifiable incident **immediately** after becoming aware of it.
- **s. 39** — Duty to preserve the incident site until an inspector arrives (or a longer period is directed).

The regulator to be notified is **WorkSafe WA** (notify by phone — 1800 678 198 — or online via the Safety Regulation System, SRS).

### 1.7 Offence and penalty framework (Part 2, Division 5)

The WHS Act creates a tiered offence structure, in addition to the civil tools of improvement notices, prohibition notices and enforceable undertakings.

- **s. 30A — Industrial manslaughter (crime).** Committed where a PCBU (or an officer of a PCBU) engages in conduct causing a person's death, in breach of a health and safety duty, knowing the conduct was likely to cause death or serious harm and in disregard of that likelihood. Penalty: **individual — 20 years' imprisonment and a fine of $5,000,000; body corporate — $10,000,000.** (Verified from s. 30A text.)
- **s. 31 — Category 1 offence** (reckless conduct causing death or serious harm — failure that *causes* death or serious harm). **[Penalty amounts below from WA legal-practitioner summaries; verify against ss. 31–33 text.]** Commonly cited maxima: body corporate $3,500,000; individual (PCBU/officer) $680,000 and/or 5 years' imprisonment; other individual $340,000 and/or 5 years.
- **s. 32 — Category 2 offence** (failure that *exposes* a person to risk of death, injury or harm). Commonly cited maxima: body corporate $1,800,000; PCBU/officer $350,000; other individual $170,000.
- **s. 33 — Category 3 offence** (general failure to comply with a health and safety duty). Commonly cited maxima: body corporate $570,000; officer $120,000; individual $55,000.
- **s. 34 — Exceptions** (e.g. volunteers, unincorporated associations).

## 2. Work Health and Safety (General) Regulations 2022 (WA)

**Short title:** *Work Health and Safety (General) Regulations 2022* (WA)
**Gazetted:** 11 March 2022; **commenced 31 March 2022** (in force with the WHS Act).

These Regulations apply to **all workplaces** except those covered by the Mines Regulations and the Petroleum and Geothermal Energy Operations (PAGEO) Regulations. They largely mirror the national model WHS Regulations and cover topics including:

- workplace management, facilities and first aid;
- hazardous manual tasks, falls, plant and structures;
- hazardous chemicals, asbestos, noise and confined spaces;
- high risk work licences and authorisations (a "high risk work licence" replaces the former OSH "certificate of competency" system);
- construction work and demolition;
- major hazard facilities.

**Transition.** The WHS General Regulations **repealed** the *Occupational Safety and Health Act 1984* and the *Occupational Safety and Health Regulations 1996* (from 31 March 2022). Transitional and savings provisions are in **Part 11.4** of the Regulations and in **regulation 2 (Commencement)** (which delays commencement of certain specific requirements, e.g. some asbestos provisions).

## 3. Work Health and Safety (Mines) Regulations 2022 (WA)

**Short title:** *Work Health and Safety (Mines) Regulations 2022* (WA)
**Gazetted:** 11 March 2022 (pp. 1399–2137), **SL 2022/32**; **commenced 31 March 2022**.

These Regulations apply to **mining and mineral exploration operations** (quarries included). Most provisions of the General Regulations are replicated for mining.

### 3.1 Commencement — phased provisions (regulation 2)

Regulation 2 staggers commencement:
- (a) the Regulations (other than rr. 58, 460(1), 473(2A), 475(2A), 477(1A) and 489(2)) commence on the day WHS Act **s. 276** comes into operation (31 March 2022);
- (b) r. 58 commences 2 years later;
- (c) rr. 460(1), 473(2A), 475(2A), 477(1A) and 489(2) commence 12 months later (asbestos-related requirements, e.g. 31 March 2023/2024).

Transitional and savings provisions are in **Part 11.4**.

### 3.2 The mine operator

A **mine operator** is the person (including a partnership, syndicate or other association) who:
- for an exploration-only mine — has overall control and supervision of the exploration operations and the exploration manager; and
- otherwise — is the proprietor, lessee or occupier of the mine with overall control and supervision of the mine and its mining operations. **(r. 5C — "meaning of mine operator")**

Duties of the mine operator are in **Part 10.7A, Division 2** (e.g. **r. 675ZD** information about mine operator / notification to the regulator; **r. 675ZE** duties of mine operator; **r. 675ZF**, **r. 675ZG**). The mine operator must give the regulator notice of appointment and of changes (no later than 7 days after a change).

### 3.3 Statutory positions (Part 10.7A and Schedule 26)

**Part 10.7A** prescribes **statutory positions** — appointed by the mine operator — who carry specific safety functions. Five **key statutory positions** (only one person each at a time, with alternates required):

1. **site senior executive**
2. **exploration manager**
3. **underground manager (coal)** — underground coal mines
4. **underground manager (non-coal)** — underground non-coal mines
5. **quarry manager** — where quarry operations are carried out (see **Schedule 26, clause 14**)

**Schedule 26** sets out the further statutory positions and their eligibility/qualification requirements, including: quarry managers (cl. 14), statutory supervisors, authorised mine surveyors (underground and quarry), underground supervisors, underground ventilation officers, mine air quality officers, radiation safety officers, noise officers, electrical supervisors, high-voltage operators, and winding engine drivers.

A **quarry manager** must be appointed where quarry operations are carried out and a levy is payable under the *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994*. Eligibility requires (depending on workforce size) a first class mine manager's certificate (coal or non-coal), a quarry manager's certificate, or a restricted quarry manager's certificate.

**Note on "registered manager":** the term **"registered manager"** was used under the repealed *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994* regime (replaced by the **site senior executive** role under the WHS Mines Regulations). **[If you need the precise MSIA 1994 "registered manager" definition/status, cite the MSIA 1994 as it stood pre-2022 — clause number unverified.]**

### 3.4 Principal mining hazards and Principal Mining Hazard Management Plans (PMP)

- **r. 612** — meaning of **principal mining hazard** (an activity, process, plant, structure, substance, situation or circumstance relating to mining operations with reasonable potential to result in multiple deaths in a single incident, or a series of recurring incidents — e.g. geotechnical instability, inrush, mine shafts/winding systems, mobile plant, fire/explosion, gas outburst, spontaneous combustion).
- **r. 627** — identification of principal mining hazards and conduct of risk assessments.
- **r. 628** — preparation of a **principal mining hazard management plan (PMP)** for each identified principal mining hazard; the PMP must set out the nature of the hazard, related hazards, analysis methods, the risk assessment record, control measures, training/information arrangements, and design/engineering standards relied on. A PMP forms part of the **mine safety management system** (see r. 622(1)(d)(i)).
- **r. 629** — review of PMPs.

### 3.5 Mine safety management system (MSMS)

- **r. 621** — duty of the mine operator to establish and implement a **mine safety management system (MSMS)**.
- **r. 622** — required content of the MSMS (including, among other things, the principal mining hazard management plans).
- **r. 623** — performance standards and audit; **r. 625** — review; **r. 625A** — consultation.

### 3.6 "Safe work instrument" — clarification

The task brief refers to "the safe work instrument." **There is no "safe work instrument" in the Western Australian WHS regime** — "safe work instrument" is a **New Zealand** legislative concept (made under s. 227 of the NZ *Health and Safety at Work Act 2015*), not a WA one. The equivalent WA instruments are:

- the **mine safety management system (MSMS)** — rr. 621–625;
- the **principal mining hazard management plans (PMP)** — rr. 627–629; and
- the **statutory position** framework and associated certificates of competence — Part 10.7A and Schedule 26.

## 4. Transition from the legacy mining regime

### 4.1 Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994 (WA)

The *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994* (MSIA) and the *Mines Safety and Inspection Regulations 1995* (MSIR) were the pre-2022 WA mining-specific WHS laws. The *Work Health and Safety Act 2020* (**Part 15, Division 2, Subdivision 1**) amended the MSIA, **deleting the bulk of its operational safety provisions** on 31 March 2022 (e.g. ss. 101B–102AA deleted by No. 36 of 2020 s. 292; ss. 105–107 deleted by No. 36 of 2020 s. 297; s. 6A deleted).

**The MSIA 1994 is NOT fully repealed.** It remains in force for, at least, the **levy / funding** provisions that pay for the regulation of mine safety, including:
- **s. 105A** — Regulations for levy for costs of administering the *Work Health and Safety Act 2020* in relation to mines and mining operations;
- **s. 105AB** — Mines Safety Account;
- the *Mines Safety and Inspection Levy Regulations 2010*.

This is why WorkSafe/DMIRS note that levies "continue to be collected … under the *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994*." Do not describe the MSIA as fully repealed — its safety provisions have been superseded, but the Act itself still operates for levy and related purposes.

### 4.2 Mines Safety and Inspection Regulations 1995 (WA)

The *Mines Safety and Inspection Regulations 1995* are being phased out. Transitional and savings provisions in **Part 11.4** of the WHS (Mines) Regulations 2022 **preserve or transition** certain existing arrangements from the MSI Regulations 1995, and **regulation 2** delays commencement of specified new duties. Some MSIR 1995 requirements therefore continue to have transitional effect. **[Exact scope of MSIR 1995 provisions still having effect should be checked against Part 11.4 of the WHS Mines Regulations — specific clause numbers not individually verified here.]**

### 4.3 Summary of the transition

| Before 31 March 2022 | After 31 March 2022 |
|---|---|
| *Occupational Safety and Health Act 1984* | Repealed — replaced by *Work Health and Safety Act 2020* |
| *Occupational Safety and Health Regulations 1996* | Repealed — replaced by WHS (General) Regulations 2022 |
| *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994* (safety provisions) | Operational safety provisions deleted; Act remains for levy/funding provisions |
| *Mines Safety and Inspection Regulations 1995* | Superseded by WHS (Mines) Regulations 2022; transitional provisions (Part 11.4) preserve some arrangements |

## 5. Codes of practice (DMIRS / WorkSafe WA)

A **code of practice** is a practical guide to achieving the standard required under WHS law. It is not itself legislation, but is admissible in court as evidence of what is known about a hazard/risk and may be used to assess compliance. Transition arrangements let codes approved under the *Occupational Safety and Health Act 1984* and *Mines Safety and Inspection Act 1994* continue in effect as if approved by the Minister under the WHS Act.
