# Mine & Industrial Hazard Controls — Governing Standards and Regulations (Western Australia)

> **Purpose.** Precision reference for a safety-advisor bot serving WA industrial and mining
> workers. Every standard, regulation, year and clause number below has been verified against
> an authoritative source (Standards Australia store, WorkSafe WA / commerce.wa.gov.au, DMIRS,
> WA legislation on legislation.wa.gov.au and AustLII). Where a clause number could not be
> verified to a source document it is marked **[clause unverified]** and must not be quoted as
> fact until confirmed.
>
> **Two governing instruments in WA.** For general industry the **Work Health and Safety
> (General) Regulations 2022** apply; for mining the **Work Health and Safety (Mines)
> Regulations 2022** apply. Where regulation numbers coincide they are shown as
> `General r. X / Mines r. X`. Both sit under the **Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA)**.
>
> **The control hierarchy (all hazards).** The WHS Regulations require a duty holder to work
> through the hierarchy when a risk cannot be eliminated, in this order:
> 1. **Eliminate** — remove the hazard entirely (always the first duty).
> 2. **Substitute / Isolate / Engineering controls** — replace the hazard, separate it from
>    people, or use physical controls at the source.
> 3. **Administrative controls** — procedures, training, supervision, signage, permits.
> 4. **PPE** — last resort only; controls the harm at the worker, not the source.
> The hierarchy is given effect by WHS General Regulations r. 34 / Mines r. 34 (hierarchy of
> control measures) **[clause unverified — regulation number to be confirmed against the WA
> consolidation; the hierarchy duty is in Part 3.1 "Managing risks to health and safety"]**.

## 1. Energy isolation / Lock-out tag-out (LOTO)

**Hazard.** Stored and live energy (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, mechanical, thermal,
gravity, chemical, radiation) released during inspection, cleaning, maintenance or repair of
plant, causing crush, amputation, shock, arc flash, burns or death.

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — design plant so maintenance can be done without stored-energy exposure.
- **Isolate (primary control)** — isolate and lock out **all** energy sources; de-energise and
  dissipate stored energy.
- **Engineering** — positive isolation devices (lockable isolators, lockout circuit breakers,
  valves, blanks/spades, hasps).
- **Administrative** — written isolation procedure, personal danger tags, permit-to-work,
  "authorised person" / "competent person" appointment.
- **PPE** — only as residual protection (arc-rated PPE, insulating gloves) during verified work.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **AS/NZS 4836:2023** — *Safe working on or near low-voltage and extra-low voltage electrical
  installations and equipment* (supersedes AS/NZS 4836:2011). Provides the de-energisation,
  testing and lockout principles for electrical isolation; the "**test before you touch**" rule.
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022** — plant duties:
  - r. 203 — management of risks to health and safety (plant).
  - r. 204 — control of risks (guarding / protection of persons from moving parts).
  - r. 210 — operational controls (operator controls lockable into "off").
  - r. 211 — emergency stop devices **[clause unverified]**.
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 Part 4.7 — Electrical safety and energised electrical
  work** (r. 146–165):
  - r. 146 — meaning of **electrical work** (verified via WorkSafe WA overview).
  - r. 148 — **energised electrical work** requirements / risk assessment (verified via WA
    Government "Safe working guidelines for electrical workers").
  - Energised electrical work is **prohibited** except in the narrow exceptions; equipment must
    be de-energised, isolation points physically secured, and **proven de-energised** before work.
- **Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991 (WA)** — r. 49 and r. 55 govern electrical work on
  or near energised installations (r. 55 prohibits live work except prescribed circumstances).
- **Mines:** DMIRS guideline *Isolation of hazardous energies associated with plant in Western
  Australian mining operations* — the "**lock, tag and try**" principle; isolation by a competent
  person; personal locks at each isolation point.
- **WorkSafe WA** — Guidance note *Isolation of plant* and checklist *Isolation of plant /
  lock-out tag-out*.

**Key method — "lock, tag, try".** Shut down → identify all energy sources → isolate → dissipate
stored energy → lock out every isolation point (each worker's own lock) → attach personal danger
tag → **try** to re-start plant to prove isolation effective, **before** work begins.

## 2. Confined space entry

**Hazard.** Enclosed/partially enclosed space not designed for human occupancy with risk of
unsafe oxygen, toxic or flammable atmosphere, or engulfment.

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — do the work without entering (remote inspection, cleaning devices, long tools).
- **Substitute/Isolate** — isolate connected plant and services (r. 70).
- **Engineering** — forced ventilation, purging, fixed access.
- **Administrative** — confined space **entry permit** (r. 67), signage (r. 68), stand-by person /
  communication & safety monitoring (r. 69), atmospheric testing and monitoring (r. 71, r. 72),
  emergency/rescue procedures (r. 74), training (r. 76).
- **PPE** — air-supplied RPE where a safe atmosphere cannot be guaranteed.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **AS/NZS 2865:2009** — *Confined spaces* (originated as AS 2865–2009; redesignated joint
  standard incorporating Amendment No 1, October 2025). Sets minimum requirements for a safe
  system of entry, risk assessment, atmospheric monitoring, training and permits. Note: this
  standard **does not cover underground mining** (other systems apply — see §4).
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 Part 4.3 (r. 62–77) / Mines Regulations 2022 Part 4.3
  (same numbering):**
  - r. 66 — managing risks to health and safety (risk assessment by a **competent person**).
  - r. 67 — **confined space entry permit** (must be completed before entry).
  - r. 68 — signage.
  - r. 69 — communication and safety monitoring.
  - r. 70 — specific control: connected plant and services (isolation).
  - r. 71 — specific control: **atmosphere** (safe oxygen level; airborne contaminants below
    exposure standard; purging/ventilation; no oxygen >21 % by volume for purging).
  - r. 72 — specific control: **flammable gases and vapours** (see LEL thresholds below).
  - r. 73 — specific control: fire and explosion.
  - r. 74 — emergency procedures.
  - r. 77 — entry permit and risk assessment must be kept (≥28 days; 2 years after a notifiable
    incident).

**Gas testing / atmosphere thresholds (r. 71–72).**
- Test for, in order: **oxygen → flammable gases/vapours → toxic contaminants**, using a
  **calibrated** gas detector, from **outside** the space first, then at multiple levels
  (top/middle/bottom, side to side).
- **Flammable gas LEL rule (r. 72):** keep concentration **< 5 % of LEL**; if **5 %–10 % of
  LEL** remove workers unless a **calibrated continuous-monitoring flammable gas detector** is
  in use; at **≥ 10 % of LEL** remove workers immediately.
- A person's senses must never be used to judge whether an atmosphere is safe.

## 3. Working at height / fall protection

**Hazard.** Falls from any height where a person could be injured (WA does not prescribe a
general trigger height for most work — the risk, not a fixed threshold, governs).

**Control hierarchy (regulation r. 79 mirrors the hierarchy).**
- **Eliminate** — work at ground level.
- **Fall prevention device** (primary) — secure fence, edge protection, working platform, cover.
- **Work positioning system** (secondary) — restrains the worker from reaching a fall edge.
- **Fall arrest system** (last) — harness, lanyard/energy absorber, anchor, arrest device;
  requires rescue plan.
- **Administrative** — SWMS, training, permit systems, ladder safe-use.
- **PPE** — the fall-arrest harness itself is PPE, used only when higher controls are not
  reasonably practicable.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 Part 4.4 — Falls:**
  - r. 78 — management of risk of fall.
  - r. 79 — specific requirements to minimise risk of fall (fall prevention device → work
    positioning → fall arrest). For high-risk construction work r. 79 commenced **1 October 2026**
    (previously OSH Regs 1996 r. 3.55 applied, which specified 2 m / 3 m edge triggers).
  - r. 79A — duties as to holes or openings in floors.
  - r. 80 — emergency and rescue procedures (must be established and **tested** where a fall
    arrest system is used; prompt rescue to avoid suspension intolerance).
- **AS/NZS 1891 series — *Industrial fall-arrest systems and devices* / *Personal equipment for
  work at height*:**
  - AS/NZS 1891.1:2020 — Part 1: Manufacturing requirements for full-body/combination/lower-body
    harnesses.
  - AS/NZS 1891.2:2001 — Part 2: Horizontal lifeline and rail systems.
  - AS/NZS 1891.3:2020 — Part 3: Fall-arrest devices.
  - **AS/NZS 1891.4:2025** — Part 4: Selection, use and maintenance (current edition; supersedes
    1891.4:2009). Anchorage, fall clearance, inspection and rescue requirements.
- **AS/NZS 1657** — Fixed platforms, walkways, stairways and ladders (referenced design
  standard).
- Code of Practice: *Managing the risk of falls at workplaces* (WorkSafe WA / Safe Work
  Australia).

## 4. Hazardous atmosphere / gas testing in mines

**Hazard.** Oxygen deficiency, toxic gases (e.g. CO, H₂S, NO₂ after blasting), flammable gases
(CH₄/coal, other hydrocarbons), dust and heat/humidity in underground and surface mines.

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — eliminate the source (ventilation to remove/ dilute contaminants).
- **Engineering** — mine ventilation system, remote monitoring.
- **Administrative** — air monitoring programs, statutory officers, gas-monitoring plans,
  withdrawal/re-entry procedures, permits.
- **PPE** — escape respirators, self-rescuers (e.g. self-contained self-rescuer in coal mines).

**Who may test / monitor.**
- Air monitoring in a mine must be carried out by a **competent person**; for underground mines
  where compliance with air-quality requirements is uncertain, monitoring is carried out by a
  **mine air quality officer (MAQO)** or **underground ventilation officer (UVO)**, or a
  competent person under their supervision (Mines Regulations r. 648) **[r. 648 verified via
  Mines Regs text]**.
- **Mine Air Quality Officer (MAQO)** is a **statutory position** under **Mines Regulations 2022,
  Schedule 26, clause 4**. A MAQO **must** be appointed where an identified hazard includes
  exposure to airborne contaminants, **heat or humidity**. Functions: advise on reducing
  exposure; plan and carry out air-quality/contaminant monitoring; report results.
- **Eligibility (Schedule 26 cl. 4(3) / r. 675ZZK(1)(c)):** complete a regulator-approved course
  in sampling and assessing airborne contaminants, an approved WHS risk-management unit, and
  pass the applicable legislation examination.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (Mines) Regulations 2022:**
  - r. 50 / Mines r. 59 — air monitoring of airborne contaminants by a competent person (General
    r. 50; Mines numbering r. 59) **[verify General vs Mines cross-number against consolidation]**.
  - r. 648 — air monitoring in underground mines (MAQO / UVO / supervised competent person).
  - Schedule 26 cl. 4 + r. 675ZZK — MAQO appointment and course approval.
- **AS 2865 / AS/NZS 2865** does not apply underground (see §2); mining atmospheres are governed
  by the Mines Regulations and mine ventilation / gas-monitoring plans.
- Gas detection equipment used to comply must be suitable, calibrated and maintained; continuous
  flammable-gas monitoring thresholds mirror the LEL rules in §2 where confined-space-like work
  occurs.

## 5. Hazardous manual tasks / manual handling

**Hazard.** Musculoskeletal disorders (MSD) from lifting, lowering, pushing, pulling, carrying,
holding or restraining involving repetitive/sustained or high/sudden force, repetitive movement,
sustained/awkward posture, or vibration.

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — automate or remove the task.
- **Substitute** — lighter loads, smaller packaging, better load shapes.
- **Isolate** — separate worker from the load/hazard.
- **Engineering** — mechanical aids (hoists, trolleys, adjustable workstations), load redesign.
- **Administrative** — job rotation, rest breaks, training in safe handling technique, warm-up.
- **PPE** — gloves for grip/cold (does **not** address the biomechanical risk; last resort).

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 Part 4.2 / Mines Regulations 2022 Part 4.2:**
  - r. 60 — managing risks to health and safety (MSD associated with a hazardous manual task;
    r. 60(2) lists matters to have regard to: postures, movements, forces, vibration, duration,
    frequency, environment, work-area design, systems of work, nature/size/weight of load).
  - r. 61 — duties of designers, manufacturers, importers and suppliers.
- **Code of Practice:** *Hazardous manual tasks* (WorkSafe WA, approved under WHS Act s. 274).
- **No single AS/NZS standard governs manual handling.** (The former *National Code of Practice
  for Manual Handling* [NOHSC:2005(1990)] was superseded by the WHS Code of Practice; there is no
  mandatory numeric weight limit in WA — the test is risk-based.) **[clause unverified — no
  numeric limit exists; confirm no WA-specific weight limit before asserting one]**.

## 6. Machine guarding

**Hazard.** Contact with moving parts, ejected workpieces/materials, in-running nip points,
entanglement, shearing, crushing, cutting.

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — design out the hazard.
- **Engineering (primary)** — fixed guards; where access is needed during operation use
  **interlocked** guards; physical barriers removable only with a tool; presence-sensing
  safeguarding systems; emergency stop.
- **Administrative** — lockout for maintenance, training, safe systems of work.
- **PPE** — last resort.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **AS/NZS 4024 series — *Safety of machinery* (26 parts, European/ISO-based):**
  - AS/NZS 4024.1201:2014 — general principles for design — risk assessment and risk reduction.
  - AS/NZS 4024.1601:2014 — design of controls, interlocks and guarding — **guards** (fixed and
    movable).
  - AS/NZS 4024.1602:2014 — **interlocking devices** associated with guards.
  - AS 4024.1603:2019 — prevention of **unexpected start-up**.
  - AS/NZS 4024.1604:2019 — **emergency stop** — principles for design (ISO 13850:2017 MOD).
  - (Series 3: specific machines — power presses, robots, conveyors; e.g. AS/NZS 4024.3611:2015
    belt conveyors.)
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 / Mines Regulations 2022:**
  - r. 203 — management of risks to health and safety (plant).
  - r. 204 — control of risks (guarding): where guarding is used it must be a permanently fixed
    barrier, an interlocked physical barrier, a tool-removable barrier, or (last) a
    presence-sensing safeguarding system; guard must resist impact, prevent bypassing, and plant
    must not restart unless guarding is replaced.
- Code of Practice: *Managing the risks of plant in the workplace* (WorkSafe WA).

## 7. Personal protective equipment (PPE)

**Position in the hierarchy.** PPE is the **lowest / last-resort control**. It does not control
the hazard at source and relies on correct selection, fit, use and maintenance. It must only be
relied on where higher-order controls are not reasonably practicable, and must be provided
**in addition to** (not instead of) higher controls.

**General duty.** The PCBU must provide suitable PPE to workers **unless the risk has been
otherwise controlled**; PPE must be provided free of charge, maintained, replaced, and workers
trained in its proper use and storage.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 / Mines Regulations 2022 — Division 5, Part 3.2:**
  - r. 44 — provision to workers and use of PPE.
  - r. 45 — PPE used by other persons.
  - r. 46 — duties of worker (use/wear PPE as instructed).
  - r. 47 — duty of person other than worker.
- **Key equipment standards (for selection, where relevant):**
  - AS/NZS 1801 — occupational protective helmets.
  - AS/NZS 1336 — eye and face protection — guidelines; AS/NZS 1337 — eye protectors.
  - AS/NZS 2210 — safety, protective and occupational footwear.
  - AS/NZS 2161 — occupational protective gloves.
  - AS/NZS 1716 — respiratory protective devices.
  - AS/NZS 1270 — acoustics — hearing protectors (see §8).

## 8. Noise

**Hazard.** Noise-induced hearing loss from occupational noise exposure.

**Exposure standard.** Defined in WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 **r. 56** as:
- **L_Aeq,8h = 85 dB(A)** (8-hour equivalent continuous A-weighted sound pressure level), **or**
- **L_C,peak = 140 dB(C)** (C-weighted peak).
- Determined in accordance with **AS/NZS 1269.1:2005** (*Occupational noise management —
  Measurement and assessment of noise immission and exposure*).

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — remove the noisy process.
- **Substitute** — quieter equipment/process.
- **Engineering** — enclosures, silencers, isolation, damping, noise barriers.
- **Administrative** — reduced exposure time, hearing-conservation zones, signage.
- **PPE** — hearing protectors (earmuffs/plugs) **last**, provided when engineering/administrative
  controls cannot get exposure below the standard.

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 Part 4.1 / Mines Regulations 2022 Part 4.1:**
  - r. 56 — meaning of exposure standard for noise (85 dB(A) / 140 dB(C)).
  - r. 57 — managing risk of hearing loss from noise (must not exceed the exposure standard,
    so far as reasonably practicable).
  - r. 58 — **audiometric testing** (in force from 31 March 2024: where workers are provided and
    frequently required to use hearing protection to control exposure above the standard, provide
    audiometric testing within 3 months of start and at least every 2 years; more frequent where
    L_Aeq,8h ≥ 100 dB(A)).
  - r. 59 — duties of designers, manufacturers, importers and suppliers of plant.
- **AS/NZS 1269 series — *Occupational noise management*:** Part 0 (overview), Part 1:2005
  (measurement and assessment), Part 2 (noise control management), Part 3 (hearing protector
  program), Part 4 (audiological assessment).
- **AS/NZS 1270** — hearing protectors (selection/attenuation).

## 9. Heat stress / thermal work

**Hazard.** Heat illness (heat stroke, exhaustion, dehydration) and cold stress from working in
extreme thermal environments — surface and underground (deep mines have high geothermal heat and
humidity).

**Control hierarchy.**
- **Eliminate** — remove the heat source / schedule work outside peak heat.
- **Substitute/Isolate** — cooler process; isolate radiant heat sources.
- **Engineering** — ventilation, air cooling, shielding/reflective barriers, mechanical aids to
  reduce exertion.
- **Administrative** — work/rest cycles, hydration, acclimatisation, monitoring (WBGT or
  equivalent), self-pacing, task rotation, first-aid/heat-illness response.
- **PPE** — cooling vests, breathable clothing (last resort; does not lower core risk alone).

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 r. 41A — duty to protect from extremes of heat and cold**
  (Part 3.2, Division 2, general working environment) **[r. 41A verified via WA General Regs
  contents/consolidation]**.
- **Mines:** a **Mine Air Quality Officer** must be appointed where a hazard includes exposure to
  **heat or humidity** (Mines Regulations 2022, Schedule 26 cl. 4 — see §4); thermal environment
  is managed within the mine safety management system / health management plan.
- **Guidance (non-statutory):** Safe Work Australia *Managing the risks of working in heat*.
- **No mandatory numeric heat-exposure standard is set in the WA Regulations**; heat is managed
  as a risk under the general duty (r. 34 / Part 3.1). Measurement instruments such as the WBGT
  (wet-bulb globe temperature, ISO 7243) are used as an assessment tool **[clause unverified —
  ISO 7243 reference not confirmed against WA instruments]**.

## 10. Emergency response and fire

**Hazard.** Fire, explosion, toxic release and other emergencies requiring planned response,
evacuation, first aid and first-response firefighting.

**Control hierarchy (emergency controls).**
- **Eliminate/Prevent** — control ignition sources, housekeeping, hazardous-area zoning.
- **Engineering** — detection/alarm systems, sprinklers, emergency lighting, exit routes, fixed
  suppression.
- **Administrative** — **emergency plan** (r. 43), Emergency Control Organisation, evacuation
  drills, training.
- **First-response equipment** — portable fire extinguishers, fire blankets (selection, location,
  maintenance to standards).

**Governing standards / regulations.**
- **WA WHS (General) Regulations 2022 / Mines Regulations 2022:**
  - r. 42 — duty to provide first aid.
  - r. 43 — duty to prepare, maintain and implement an **emergency plan**.
  - (Mines: additional emergency-management duties apply to mine operators — mine emergency
    plans, mine emergency workers, self-rescuers.)
- **Firefighting equipment standards:**
  - **AS/NZS 1841 series — *Portable fire extinguishers*:** Part 1 (general), Part 2 (water),
    Part 3 (wet chemical), Part 4 (foam), Part 5 (powder), Part 6 (CO₂), Part 7 (vaporising
    liquid), Part 8 (non-rechargeable). Manufacture also subject to the ACCC mandatory safety
    standard (Consumer Goods (Portable Non-aerosol Fire Extinguishers) Safety Standard 2021).
  - **AS 2444:2001** — *Portable fire extinguishers and fire blankets — Selection and location*
    (current, reconfirmed 2021).
  - **AS 1851** — *Routine service of fire protection systems and equipment* (maintenance).
  - **AS/NZS 3504** — fire blankets.
- **Emergency planning standard:**
  - **AS 3745:2010** — *Planning for emergencies in facilities* (emergency plan, Emergency
    Control Organisation, training) (current edition; Amd 2:2018).
  - **AS/NZS 2293.1** — emergency escape lighting and exit signs.
