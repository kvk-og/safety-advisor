# Western Australian Electrical Licensing & Energy-Safety Regulation

**Precision note (read first).** Every regulation number below was verified against the
current *official version* of the relevant instrument on `legislation.wa.gov.au`, or against
Building and Energy / DMIRS–LGIRS guidance. Clause numbers that could not be confirmed
verbatim are marked `[clause unverified]`. Do not quote any other regulation number for WA
electrical work from this document.

**Two corrections to common errors (do not propagate):**

1. The correct instrument is the **Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991 (WA)** — made
   under the *Electricity Act 1945 (WA)*. WA does **not** have a separate instrument titled
   "Electrical (Licensing) Regulations 1991". Any reference to "Electrical (Licensing)
   Regulations" should be read as *Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991*.
2. WA does **not** have an "Electrical Safety Act 1945". The *Electricity Safety Act 1945*
   was a **New South Wales** Act (formerly the NSW *Electricity Act 1945*), since repealed.
   WA's principal electrical-safety statute is the **Electricity Act 1945 (WA)**, supplemented
   by the *Energy Coordination Act 1994 (WA)*, the *Electricity Industry Act 2004 (WA)* and
   their regulations.

## 1. Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991 (WA)

- **Citation / commencement:** Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991, made under the
  *Electricity Act 1945 (WA)*; commenced **1 November 1991** (r. 1, r. 2).
- **Current official version:** "As at 01 Jul 2026, Official Version [PCO 07-ai0-00]"
  (the most recent compilation on legislation.wa.gov.au at time of writing).

### 1.1 Definition of "electrical work" — r. 4A

`r. 4A(1)` — *electrical work* means work on electrical machines or instruments, on an
electrical installation, or on electrical appliances/equipment to which electricity is
supplied or intended to be supplied at a **nominal pressure exceeding 50 V AC or 120 V
ripple-free DC**, plus work comprising an assessment of an installation for compliance.
`r. 4A(2)` confirms it is immaterial whether the thing is connected to distribution works or
private generating plant, or whether supply is via a plug socket.

### 1.2 Licence classes — r. 20 (electrical worker's licence)

`r. 20(1)` — an electrical worker's licence is endorsed, according to the applicant's
qualifications, as **one of three classes**:

1. **electrician's licence**;
2. **electrician's training licence**; or
3. **restricted licence**.

Effect of each class:

- `r. 20(2a)` — a licence endorsed as an **electrician's licence** authorises the holder to
  carry out **electrical installing work** and **electrical fitting work**.
- `r. 20(3)(a)` — work authorised by an **electrician's licence** may be carried out
  **without supervision**.
- `r. 20(3)(b)` — work authorised by an **electrician's training licence** must be
  **effectively supervised in accordance with r. 50(4)** by the holder of an electrician's
  licence.
- `r. 20(4)` — a **restricted licence** authorises electrical work (including testing)
  associated with or for the purposes of the holder's trade or calling.
- `r. 20(5)` — a **restricted licence does NOT authorise installation or alteration of fixed
  wiring.**

Legacy grade equivalents (informational): the former "A" grade licence = electrician's
licence; "C" grade licence = electrician's training licence (transitional r. 67, inserted
31 Dec 2007).

### 1.3 Who may carry out electrical work — r. 19

`r. 19(1)` — a person who carries out electrical work commits an **offence** unless the work
is authorised by a **licence or permit**.

`r. 19(2)` lists the exceptions (work *not* requiring a licence), including (paraphrased,
refer to the source for exact conditions):

- communications/computer/radio-TV work (except supply circuits >50 V AC / 120 V DC) — `r. 19(2)(a)`;
- mechanical assembly/winding and factory manufacture — `r. 19(2)(b)`;
- authorised linework for a major network operator — `r. 19(2)(c)`;
- cable jointing by trained persons — `r. 19(2)(d)`;
- service apparatus work authorised by the relevant network operator — `r. 19(2)(e)`;
- affixing a plug/flexible-cord connector ≤1 000 V AC / 1 500 V DC — `r. 19(2)(h)`;
- testing and servicing of plug-connected appliances / portable boards / RCDs — `r. 19(2)(ia)`;
- training under supervision for a restricted licence — `r. 19(2)(i)`;
- educational/training courses — `r. 19(2)(j)`;
- work declared by the Director by Gazette order — `r. 19(2)(k)`;
- electric fences for security/stock control (but not the supply circuit to them) — `r. 19(2)(l)`;
- law-enforcement work under an approved safety management plan — `r. 19(2)(m)`;
- inspection/measurement/commissioning by a professionally qualified engineer — `r. 19(2)(n)`;
- modular wiring systems in office furniture (approved/certified systems) — `r. 19(2)(o)`;
- removal of wiring under a demolition permit (supplies removed/permanently disconnected) — `r. 19(2)(p)`;
- a supervised apprentice under a registered training contract (within the first 60 days) — `r. 19(2)(q)`.

### 1.4 Supervision rules — r. 50

- `r. 49D` — defines **levels of supervision** of electrical work (`[clause unverified]` for the
  exact level descriptors; refer to r. 49D as published).
- `r. 50` — sets out the **requirements for supervision** of electrical work; `r. 50(4)` is the
  provision invoked by r. 20(3)(b) requiring an electrician's training licence to be
  effectively supervised by an electrician's licence holder.
- `r. 50AA` — apprentices/trainees must inform the employer and supervisor of their
  experience level.

### 1.5 Licence vs permit — r. 21

A **licence** is the enduring authorisation issued under Part 3 (and Part 4 for contractors).
A **permit** is a separate, typically more limited authorisation:

- `r. 21(1)` — a permit authorises the holder, subject to specified restrictions and
  conditions, to carry out the electrical work that *would* be authorised by a licence of the
  corresponding kind.
- `r. 21(2)` — a permit may be issued subject to **supervision conditions more stringent**
  than those applying to the corresponding licence.

(In practice, permits are commonly used for recognition pathways — e.g. interstate/overseas
or gap-training applicants — and are issued for a limited period. Building and Energy lists
permits as valid for **2 years**, versus 1 or 5 years for worker licences and 1 year for
contractor licences.)

### 1.6 Electrical contractors & in-house work — Part 4 (r. 33–47)

- `r. 33` — electrical contracting prohibited unless authorised (electrical contractor's
  licence or in-house electrical installing work licence).
- `r. 34` — offence to contract with an unlicensed person for electrical installing work.
- `r. 36` — eligibility for an electrical contractor's licence (includes the prescribed
  insurance requirement — see `r. 36(1)(c)`, `(2)(c)`, `(3)(c)`).
- `r. 37` — in-house electrical installing work, when permitted.
- `r. 38` — nominees; an electrical contractor's licence requires a nominated licensed
  electrician.
- `r. 44A` — Board may require details of the contractor's insurance.

### 1.7 Notifiable work, notices & electrical safety certificates — Part 5 (r. 49–52C)

**Notifiable work** is defined in r. 3 (definition) as electrical installing work other than:
maintenance work (unless disconnection/reconnection or service-apparatus replacement);
addition/alteration of one final sub-circuit; or alteration of one or more final
sub-circuits.

- `r. 51` — **Preliminary notice** of notifiable work must be delivered to the network
  operator (or to the Director where the installation is not network-supplied) **before**
  the work commences.
- `r. 52` — **Notice of completion** must be delivered to the relevant network operator
  within **3 working days** of completion of notifiable work.
- `r. 52B` — **Electrical safety certificate** must be provided to the person for whom the
  electrical installing work was carried out within **28 days** of completion. `r. 52B(4)`
  specifies when the certificate is taken to be completed `[clause unverified — day count and
  sub-clause confirmed from Building and Energy guidance; verify against r. 52B text]`.
- `r. 52BA` — transportable structures; when compliance with r. 51, 52, 52B is not required.
- `r. 52C` — electrical contractor's duties as to electrical installing work.
- `r. 53` — unlicensed persons must not be employed/engaged for electrical installing work.
- `r. 62` — electrical workers must report unsafe electrical installations.
- `r. 63` — electrical accidents must be reported.

**Retention:** the electrical contractor must retain a copy of each electrical safety
certificate for **5 years** (Building and Energy guidance, r. 52B).

### 1.8 Standards for electrical work — Schedule 2

Schedule 2 prescribes the standards to which electrical work must comply, including the
**Australian/New Zealand Wiring Rules = AS/NZS 3000:2018** (defined in r. 3). Electrical
installing work must comply with the applicable standards.

### 1.9 Recent additions (CPR) — Division 2, r. 32A–32I

New provisions (inserted by SL 2024/211 and SL 2026/67) require licensed electricians to
complete **approved CPR courses** on issue/renewal of an endorsed electrician's licence,
with powers for the Director to approve longer completion periods and grant exemptions.

## 2. The Regulator — Building and Energy / Director of Energy Safety

- **Statutory office:** the **Director of Energy Safety** is established by the
  **Energy Coordination Act 1994 (WA), s. 5** ("A Director of Energy Safety is to be
  appointed under Part 3 of the *Public Sector Management Act 1994*"). The Director's
  functions are vested by s. 7, including functions under the *Electricity Act 1945* and the
  *Energy Coordination Act 1994*.
- **Administrative home:** the Director operates within **Building and Energy**, a division of
  the department. The department was **DMIRS** (Department of Mines, Industry Regulation and
  Safety) and, following machinery-of-government changes in the mid-2020s, is now the
  **Department of Local Government, Industry Regulation and Safety (LGIRS)**. (Both names
  appear across current WA Government pages; treat "DMIRS" and "LGIRS" as referring to the
  same portfolio for energy-safety purposes, with LGIRS being current.)
- **Branding:** Building and Energy's electrical-safety function is publicly branded
  **Energy Safety WA** (formerly `energysafety.wa.gov.au`).
- **Role:** administer the *Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991*, maintain the licence
  register, run the eNotice portal for preliminary notices / notices of completion /
  electrical safety certificates, conduct inspections, and prosecute breaches (e.g. the
  Building and Energy / Energy Safety enforcement of unsupervised-apprentice and inaccurate
  certificate offences under the *Energy Coordination Act 1994*).
- **Electrical Licensing Board:** established by r. 4 of the Regulations (8 members,
  r. 5); issues licences and permits (r. 13(2)); subject to the Director's directions
  (r. 13(3)). Disciplinary action is taken via the State Administrative Tribunal (r. 31,
  r. 47).

## 3. Residual Current Devices (RCDs / "safety switches") — Electricity Regulations 1947 (WA)

RCD requirements are in **Electricity Regulations 1947 (WA), Part IV — Residual current
devices, r. 12–18** (Part IV heading inserted 8 May 2009). Key provisions:

- `r. 12A` — **Installation of residual current devices**: RCDs are "properly installed" if,
  among other things, each complies with **AS/NZS 3190:2016**, has a **maximum rated residual
  current of 30 mA**, protects **not more than 3 final subcircuits**, and (where 2 or more
  RCDs) lighting final subcircuits are **distributed between the devices**.
- `r. 13` — **Residential premises occupied by the owner**: the owner must ensure **at least
  2 RCDs** are installed before the events specified in the regulation.
- `r. 14` — **Residential premises not occupied by the owner** (rental/lease): the owner must
  ensure **at least 2 RCDs** are installed.
- `r. 15A` — **New owner's obligation** to install RCDs, and right to recover costs.
- `r. 16` — **Common property** relating to residential premises: at least **1 RCD per
  switchboard** `[clause unverified — verify exact wording in r. 16]`.
- `r. 17` — exemption where the switchboard cannot accommodate 2 RCDs and an inspector
  certifies impracticability; then **1 RCD** suffices.
- `r. 18` — **Inspection and inquiry** powers.

**Practical effect (Building and Energy):** residential premises sold, rented, hired or used
for short-term rental accommodation must have at least **two RCDs** protecting final
sub-circuits to socket outlets and lighting, with no more than three final sub-circuits per
RCD and lighting divided between RCDs. The Regulations do **not** require RCDs on final
sub-circuits protecting hard-wired appliances (though new installations after 1 January 2019
under AS/NZS 3000:2018 may protect all final sub-circuits). Workplace/commercial RCD
requirements are governed under **WorkSafe (Work Health and Safety)** legislation rather than
the Electricity Regulations 1947.

## 4. Current WA electrical-safety legislation map

| Instrument | Role |
|---|---|
| **Electricity Act 1945 (WA)** | Principal Act; basis for licensing, electrical safety, prescribed appliances |
| **Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991 (WA)** | Licensing of electrical workers & contractors; notices; safety certificates |
| **Electricity Regulations 1947 (WA)** | Technical/safety regulation incl. Part IV RCDs; declared apparatus |
| **Electricity (Network Safety) Regulations 2015 (WA)** | Network safety and network operators |
| **Energy Coordination Act 1994 (WA)** | Establishes the Director of Energy Safety; enforcement |
| **Electricity Industry Act 2004 (WA)** | Industry/structural regulation; exemptions |
| **Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA) / WHS (Mines) Regs 2022** | Workplace electrical safety (incl. workplace RCDs) |

> No "Electrical Safety Act 1945" exists in WA. (The similarly named Act was NSW, now
> repealed.)
